# Research options for a separate Tradecraft extension

Prepared 21 October 2026. This is a literature review and proposed experimental design, not a record of new training results. No existing training code, model, split, dependency declaration or report was edited. Candidate rankings are judgments about project fit; they are not predicted performance gains.

## Recommendation

**Claim FedNTD as the first self-contained algorithm extension.** It tests whether local knowledge distillation improves the current FedAvg control while retaining its small inference model and communication schedule. **Choose a synchronization-interval study instead if the aim is to improve the completed shared-Adam method's main cost.** FedDecorr is the next inexpensive mechanism to investigate, after checking whether the learned representations actually collapse.

Do not begin with a combination of all three. A single change with an exact baseline bridge makes a useful positive or negative result possible. These are established research methods being evaluated in a new application; implementing them does not establish algorithmic novelty.

The scope below remains eight-class classification, a single global model, simulated clients, flow-level features and measured host costs. No device topology, physical edge deployment or device-specific vulnerability analysis is introduced.

Three problems should be kept distinct:

1. **Local training on heterogeneous clients loses quality.** The official39 global non-IID reference is much weaker than the centralized reference. The gap affects DoS, Recon and Spoofing as well as rare categories.
2. **The successful shared-gradient remedy synchronizes frequently.** The final compressed method has 0.6325 mean confirmation macro-F1 versus 0.5187 for its matched FedAvg control and 0.6535 centrally. It uses 2,160 synchronizations versus 108 for FedAvg. Its counted application payload is approximately 240.26 MB versus 88.06 MB for FedAvg; compression saved bytes relative to the much larger uncompressed shared-gradient reference, not relative to FedAvg.
3. **Minority recognition and alarm control remain weak even centrally.** Compressed shared Adam has mean Web-based/Brute Force category recalls of 9.87%/26.28%; the central reference has 15.60%/30.00%. The compressed model's mean actual benign false alerts are 5.36% after calibration to a 5% development target. A new FL optimizer cannot be assumed to repair a representation problem also present centrally.

These are historical measurements on their own cohort, not numerical targets that can be compared directly with a new dataset. ## 1. FedNTD: retain global knowledge during local training

**Best first isolated algorithm experiment.**

The NeurIPS 2022 paper treats local training as forgetting information outside a client's class mixture. FedNTD keeps a frozen round-start global model as a teacher. On local examples, it adds a KL distillation loss after removing the true-label logit from both teacher and student distributions. Ordinary supervised loss continues to train the true class. It needs no public auxiliary dataset. [Paper, Section 3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/fadec8f2e65f181d777507d1df69b92f-Paper-Conference.pdf).

**Why it fits here — a hypothesis.** A client dominated by DDoS or Mirai may erase useful global distinctions while taking its twenty local steps. Preserving the teacher's relative scores for the other seven classes could improve the global model without per-step communication. This has not been demonstrated for Tradecraft; the teacher could also preserve the current model's mistakes.

**Proposed adaptation.** Keep the 39 → 64 → 32 → 8 LayerNorm network, dropout 0, fixed client populations, sampling sequence, Adam reset policy, fitting-derived weighted loss and twenty-step local blocks. Add only the not-true KL term. Retain population-weighted aggregation; the paper's displayed algorithm uses uniform client averaging, so call this a Tradecraft adaptation rather than an exact reproduction. Use the [author implementation](https://github.com/Lee-Gihun/FedNTD) to resolve temperature scaling and reduction conventions before freezing the recipe.

A bounded engineering screen could use distillation strengths 0.1 and 1.0 with temperature fixed at 1, against coefficient zero. These are proposed development settings, not claimed paper optima. Keep teacher parameters detached and in evaluation mode. The coefficient-zero path must skip teacher work and reproduce the baseline exactly.

**Costs and failure modes.** Inference architecture and model-state payload per round stay unchanged. Training adds a teacher forward pass and another model copy; log this work and memory instead of calling the method free. A poorly learned rare class supplies a poor teacher. No raw-data exchange does not imply a privacy guarantee. Compare correct-category recall, wrong-attack labels and predictions of Benign separately.

**Useful outcome.** Better alarm-matched macro-F1 than matched FedAvg at the same number of rounds, with rare precision/F1 and all-class recall explicitly reviewed. This would establish an empirical training improvement, not a general solution to rare attacks.

## 2. Infrequent synchronization with a persistent server optimizer

**Best continuation of the completed shared-Adam contribution.**

FedOpt interprets aggregated client displacement as a pseudo-gradient for a server optimizer. FedAdam is an adaptive instantiation; it differs from averaging client Adam moments and from the repo's every-step shared-gradient Adam. [Adaptive Federated Optimization, Algorithms 1 and 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.00295v5).

**Question.** How much of shared Adam's quality survives when clients communicate after several local steps? This directly addresses the report's open synchronization cost. It is more informative than trying another bit width while leaving the same 2,160 barriers.

**A controlled implementation proposal.** Begin with uncompressed local SGD, no local momentum/weight decay, and the existing persistent server Adam rule. With client population weights p_i, client learning rate eta_l and K local steps, form:

`g_proxy = sum_i p_i * (theta_start - theta_client_i) / (eta_l * K)`

The K=1 update matches the aggregated data gradient mathematically, given the same batches and loss; verify tensors, Adam moments and recovery numerically. K>1 combines gradients evaluated along different local paths and is approximate. The division by eta_l*K and the repo's Adam conventions make this a **normalized FedOpt-inspired adaptation**, not a claim to reproduce published FedAdam unchanged. Do not average or reuse client Adam moments as an equivalence argument.

Use K in {1, 4, 20} as the first bounded interval comparison. Keep each client's ordered minibatch stream identical across K, rather than deriving different sample schedules from differently numbered rounds. Initially disable compression so scheduling and quantization are not changed together.

For twenty clients, batch size 500 and 21.6 million backward examples, the calculated synchronization counts are 2,160, 540 and 108 respectively. K=2 and K=8 would give 1,080 and 270, but are follow-up choices, not extra automatic trials. These are accounting identities, not measured speedups or predicted quality.

**Controls.** Include matched local-SGD/FedAvg and reset-client-Adam FedAvg references if making claims about optimizer choice. The K=1 bridge supplies the shared-gradient reference. Equal processed examples do not fix aggregate batch size, server update count or optimizer trajectory. A wall-clock or bytes-matched comparison would answer another question.

**Costs and failure modes.** Local drift may return immediately. Server/client learning rates and epsilon scaling interact with displacement normalization. Add the existing block serializer only after choosing an interval on development data; changed pseudo-gradient and broadcast semantics require a new compression bridge. A fourfold barrier reduction can be useful even if it costs a little quality, but network speed needs actual measurement or an explicitly labeled latency model.

## 3. FedDecorr: test and discourage representation collapse

**A small training-only change, justified by a diagnostic first.**

FedDecorr studies low-rank feature representations under heterogeneous FL and regularizes correlations between representation dimensions during local fitting. It changes the training objective without adding an inference head or a new message. [ICLR 2023 paper, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.00226).

**Proposed diagnostic.** Use a fixed fitting-only probe to compare centered penultimate-feature spectra for the centralized, IID and non-IID models. Examine balanced and per-class views so that different class mixtures are not mistaken for collapse. Low effective rank alone is not proof of harmful collapse: some dimensions can be legitimately redundant. Continue only if the observed representation pattern makes the mechanism plausible.

**Proposed adaptation.** Obtain the existing 32-dimensional penultimate activation through an extension wrapper or hook. Add one decorrelation term while retaining cross-entropy, client sampling and all communication settings. A coefficient-zero path must preserve logits, RNG consumption and updates exactly. A fixed coefficient screen such as 0.01 and 0.1 is an engineering proposal only.

**An implementation detail that matters.** The [authors' loss code](https://github.com/bytedance/FedDecorr/blob/master/loss.py) standardizes features, uses off-diagonal products and has batch-size-dependent normalization. A coefficient copied from a normalized correlation-matrix formula may have a different effective strength. Freeze the exact formula, variance convention, epsilon, batch size and singleton handling before interpreting results; do not silently replace correlation regularization with covariance regularization.

**Costs and failure modes.** For width d=32, correlation work scales approximately with batch size times d squared; profile it locally. There are no additional model-sized messages, and inference stays unchanged. Increasing representation rank does not necessarily separate rare attacks from benign traffic. A treatment can improve a spectrum while failing every useful classification criterion.

## 4. FedLC: train with class-dependent logit margins

**Most targeted loss alternative; more care is needed with missing classes.**

FedLC calibrates logits inside the local training objective using class-frequency-dependent pairwise margins. Its fine-grained margin scales with inverse fourth roots of class counts. This changes the gradients during fitting, unlike the repo's completed posterior prior correction or its alarm threshold. [ICML 2022 paper, Section 4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/zhang22p.html), [full derivation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/zhang22p/zhang22p.pdf).

**Why it might help.** The virtual clients have precisely the label skew the method targets. It could affect how absent/minority classifier rows change on a majority client's batches. It cannot create information about an attack for which no client has useful examples.

**Clean comparison.** First distinguish ordinary CE, the existing square-root-weighted CE, and FedLC applied to ordinary CE, all with the same model and work. Adding FedLC on top of existing weights is a further treatment and could compensate imbalance twice. Freeze the margin coefficient and its count units: counts and normalized proportions change the numerical scale.

The inverse-root rule is undefined at zero counts. Specify a floor or smoothing rule from fitting counts before running, retain all eight output classes, and label that choice as an adaptation. Verify finite gradients for absent classes and exact reduction to the chosen CE control when the margin strength is zero. Compare unadjusted inference logits under the same separately calibrated alarm rule.

**Costs and failure modes.** Local class-count bookkeeping and eight logit offsets are small. Inference architecture and FedAvg payload remain unchanged. The main risk is repeating the historical pattern: more rare predictions with poor precision, more benign alerts or worse DoS/DDoS separation. Thus this is below FedNTD in the initial shortlist, not a guaranteed rare-recall fix.

## 5. TabM: a modern tabular architecture extension

**Best separate architecture question; a larger scope than local-loss changes.**

TabM uses parameter sharing to obtain ensemble-like MLP predictions. Its ICLR 2025 evaluation covers general tabular tasks, providing a relevant alternative to moving immediately to a Transformer. It does not establish improvement on CICIoT2023 macro-F1 or under federation. [Paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c1ba41c694834aeef91ae161711d4939-Abstract-Conference.html).

**Proposed sequence.** First compare a separately named centralized TabM configuration with a freshly matched plain MLP on the same extension cohort. Freeze one modest ensemble configuration and report its actual parameter count, training cost and inference throughput. If it cannot improve the class/false-alert trade-off centrally, do not add the complexity of federation.

The [official implementation](https://github.com/yandex-research/tabm) specifies an important distinction: train by averaging the losses of individual predictions; at classification inference, normally average probabilities. Its API/defaults evolve, so pin a version or commit. Use a separately recorded alarm score for the averaged probabilities; do not assume the old logit margin transfers unchanged. New numerical feature embeddings should be a separate factor, especially because the archive already tested embeddings.

**Costs and failure modes.** More implicit members increase computation even when parameters are shared. This is a new inference model; the 5,096-parameter capacity claim no longer applies. Independent client member symmetries can also complicate naïve parameter averaging. A positive centralized result is not a positive federated result. Optional TabM dependencies should live with this extension, not alter the baseline installation.

## 6. Neyman–Pearson alarm calibration

**An alarm-reliability extension requiring no new neural training.**

The NP umbrella approach chooses a score threshold using held-out class-0 order statistics and a binomial-tail bound. Under its independence/same-distribution assumptions, it controls the probability that the *population* type-I error exceeds a chosen level. This is stronger than simply forcing an empirical calibration percentile. [Tong, Feng and Li, Science Advances 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804623/), [author paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.03109).

**Proposed adaptation.** Keep the frozen eight-class network. Use its attack-versus-benign score for the alarm decision, and retain the strongest attack category whenever the alarm passes. Fix alpha=0.05 and a violation probability such as delta=0.05 before calibration, with a benign-only calibration sample independent of model fitting and selection. Compare ordinary quantile and NP thresholds on a separate evaluation population.

**Important distinction.** The guarantee concerns population FAR with high probability over calibration sampling. It does not promise every finite future sample will have at most 5% observed false alerts. Correlated flows, duplicate groups, capture dependence or future distribution shift can invalidate the assumptions. Grouping duplicates alone does not prove i.i.d. benign observations. If suitable independent samples are unavailable, present a sensitivity experiment, not a certified network-security guarantee.

**Costs and failure modes.** It adds threshold computation but no larger inference model or training communication. More conservative thresholds generally miss more attacks. This can improve reliability of an alarm constraint while reducing macro-F1; evaluate the retained attack-category recalls and rare missed-attack rates explicitly.

## Recent papers to keep as second-stage options

- **FedRCL, CVPR 2024:** relaxed supervised contrastive learning discourages excessive within-class similarity. This is a more specific mechanism than the archived ordinary contrastive trial, but would need a plain-contrastive control and stable local positive pairs. Sparse rare labels make that challenging. [Official proceedings and code link](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Seo_Relaxed_Contrastive_Learning_for_Federated_Learning_CVPR_2024_paper.html).
- **FedWSQ, ICCV 2025:** combines weight standardization with distribution-aware nonuniform quantization. It is relevant to the existing serializer, but the paper's treatment concerns local parameter updates, not the repo's shared gradients. Test observed tensor distributions and separate quantization from weight standardization; count scale exchanges and any extra barriers. It does not solve synchronization merely by sending fewer bits. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23516), [author code](https://github.com/Seongyeol-kim/FedWSQ).
- **FedAdamom, CVPR 2026:** the author implementation describes adaptive server momentum as an alternative to adaptive learning rates, with image-classification examples. It is a recent possible round-level optimizer comparison after FedOpt controls exist. I verified the author code and its venue citation; the publisher PDF was inaccessible in this session, so its full derivation was not independently audited here. [Author implementation](https://github.com/Tenshawn/FedAdamom).

These are reading leads, not additional mandatory experiments. Mature, simpler methods can be better project choices than a newer, less directly transferable one.

## Lower-priority directions and their reasons

- **Another unchanged FedProx, SCAFFOLD, MIME, tree, routing or ordinary contrastive sweep:** overlaps completed development. A changed mechanism needs an explicit reason and a new protocol.
- **FedNova as the first equal-step experiment:** its principal target is unequal local progress. With equal-K plain SGD, its normalization can reduce to FedAvg; simply dividing an Adam displacement by a step count does not remove coordinate-wise adaptivity. [FedNova paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/564127c03caab942e503ee6f810f54fd-Abstract.html).
- **GAN/diffusion oversampling first:** the rare fitting set is small, and statistical flow features include constrained flags and linked quantities. Synthetic volume alone does not add independent attack evidence. Require validity, memorization and matched real-resampling controls before believing a gain.
- **Device GNNs or arbitrary row-sequence LSTMs:** the released inputs lack the identity/topology or verified sequence structure needed for these claims. This would reopen settled scope rather than provide a small extension.
- **Personalized client heads as the main comparison:** the current task evaluates one global classifier. Client-specific models require a separate inference-time client-ownership/evaluation design; simulated clients must not become claims about real device types.

## How to preserve the original flow

For later implementation, put new code under top-level `extensions/<topic>/`, new documentation/plans under `reports/extensions/<topic>/`, and run products under ignored `outputs/extensions/<topic>/`. These are proposed locations; this review creates no training runner.

Prefer top-level `extensions/` to adding modules inside `src/`: some existing runners hash every Python file under `src/` into their resume contract. Even adding an unused module there can change a future provenance comparison. New runners should hash both their own code and the original helpers they use.

Import the canonical labels, model and metric helpers where appropriate. Use extension-owned wrappers/losses/loops instead of editing `architectures.py`, the existing client/server apps, loss factory, experiment CLI, base requirements or frozen receipts. Never monkey-patch the original functions. Preserve data and checkpoints read-only, use fresh output destinations and give every new method an explicit report name. Keep new evidence out of historical decision selectors unless a separately reviewed adapter is added later.

For FedNTD, the first meaningful correctness checks would be: coefficient-zero identity with the current client loop; true-label exclusion and KL direction on analytical logits; detached unchanged teacher; finite missing-class behavior; unchanged upload schema; and exact checkpoint/resume including all new state. These checks would verify an implementation, not prove predictive improvement.

## A bounded experiment plan

This is a proposed ceiling, not authorization or a command to run the whole grid.

1. **Pick one owner and one question.** For the first pass, use FedAvg plus two fixed FedNTD strengths: three full endpoints, not an unrestricted optimizer/temperature/LR search.
2. **Lock data and calibration before scores.** Reused development data can support exploratory selection. Keep threshold calibration groups separate from selection groups. Do not tune on the opened official39 final test or Stage C confirmation. A reshuffle or another seed does not restore their independence. A new reserve inside historically used training data remains within-collection evidence; independent compatible captures would support a stronger generalization claim.
3. **Keep a paired control.** Same fitting rows, scaler, feature order, initialization, client assignment and local minibatch stream. For Stage C-style experiments use the 39-input LayerNorm/dropout-0 network and its fixed fitting-denominator loss contract. If using the locally available legacy46 data instead, label that as a separate legacy46 extension and rerun controls there.
4. **Record real work.** The historical 21.6M backward-example budget is a possible matched-work reference, not a required budget for every future study. Count teacher forwards, decorrelation work, optimizer updates, initialization bytes, both communication directions, synchronization barriers and measured host elapsed time separately.
5. **Predeclare useful changes.** Example engineering targets for a FedNTD screen are at least +0.02 macro-F1 over its paired FedAvg, actual FAR within 0.5 percentage points, no decrease in either rare-class F1, and a declared limit on other class-recall regressions. For an interval experiment, a different target could be at most 0.01 macro-F1 loss to its K=1 reference while reducing barriers at least fourfold and meeting predeclared FAR/class safeguards. These are new proposed targets, not existing gates or results. Fix them before running and justify changes before outcomes are observed.
6. **Stop if the question fails.** Preserve failed configurations and all class metrics. If one method passes, freeze it and add two new training seeds with its paired controls. Repeat partitions as a separately budgeted robustness check; do not select the easiest partition.

If the team explicitly chooses a broader first development screen, cap it at nine endpoints: three controls (central Adam, reset-client-Adam FedAvg, K=1 shared-gradient reference), two FedNTD strengths, two FedDecorr strengths and K=4/K=20 normalized-local-update variants. Confirming one finalist plus those three controls on two additional seeds would add eight endpoints, for seventeen total. This larger option should replace, not silently accumulate on top of, the three-endpoint first pass. FedLC, TabM and calibration studies are alternatives, not extra hidden stages.

Report macro-F1 and all eight classes' precision/recall/F1/support, benign FAR, rare attacks predicted Benign versus another attack, and measured or explicitly modeled resource costs. Show per-seed values. Sample SD over seeds is not a confidence interval for new captures. A useful negative result can explain a mechanism without winning a leaderboard.
